# Feature Registry — The 20 Rolling Features (F01–F20)

Each feature is a defined behavior, not a button. Features compose: lenses
(F01–F09) color what the instruments (F10–F13) measure, the deep interactions
(F14–F17) are the primary gestures of mastery, and the page systems (F18–F20)
are the ground everything stands on.

---

## Lenses — the nine anomaly areas as ways of seeing (F01–F09)

**F01 — Rupture Lens** (source: Internal Rupture Mechanics). Nested voice
ignition becomes visible: when a lane contains a perspective ignition, the
lens draws the seam as a fine vertical fault line across the row and tints the
ignited span. The parent text holds steady; the inner voice is legible as a
chamber inside the line.

**F02 — Collapse Lens** (source: Temporal State Collapsing). Long histories
pressed into signatures: activating this lens compresses a lane's relational
threads into a single glyph-like key floating in the gutter, with the
discarded steps shown as fading strata beneath it. The reader sees what the
key cost.

**F03 — Dissolution Lens** (source: Radial Spatial Dissolution). Thread mergers
render as confluence: the separate measures feeding a lane visibly flow into
one channel at the boundary condition, like streams joining. The merger license
(attestation / score / declared filter) is shown as a seal on the channel.

**F04 — Shift Lens** (source: Real-Time Viewpoint Shifting). Orientation
reversals draw as pivot arcs: a curved arrow across the lane marks where the
thread turned, with the distance limits that snapped into place shown as faint
brackets between the affected pools.

**F05 — Shielding Lens** (source: Dynamic Distance Shielding). Protective
detachment renders as depth-withdrawal: shielded spans sink slightly below the
page plane and desaturate, visibly removed from the threat profile while
remaining present. Declared-uncertain links show as dashed, arm's-length
tethers.

**F06 — Passivity Lens** (source: Linear Object Passivity). Payload-only
material dims to a quiet gray and flattens (depth → 0); anything capable of
execution keeps full ink and lift. The page separates, at a glance, into
participants and payload.

**F07 — Elevation Lens** (source: Gravitational Node Elevation). Hubs broadcast:
elevated records emit faint concentric rings across neighboring lanes, and the
barrier they crossed (build / render / detection threshold) is marked as a
threshold line the rings pass through. Transmission made visible.

**F08 — Convergence Lens** (source: Spatial Horizon Convergence). Footprint
matching renders as overlay ghosts: when distant regions share an identical
structural footprint, the lens superimposes their contours in the gutter as
aligned translucent shapes. No channel, no coordination — just the match.

**F09 — Rollback Lens** (source: Recursive Timeline Rollbacks). Backward trails
illuminate: activating this lens draws the reverse path a root took to its
anchor as a dotted line running against the reading direction, with discarded
forward operations shown as struck-through ghosts along the way.

## Instruments — ledger mechanics as HUD (F10–F13)

**F10 — Friction Meter.** A per-lane readout on the right margin rail: each
lane's aggregated friction coefficient as a horizontal micro-bar. Bars update
live as lenses change what counts as friction. Tapping a bar isolates the
lane's highest-friction span.

**F11 — Invertibility Audit.** A drill-down inspector: selecting any collapsed
key, merged channel, or folded structure opens a ledger-style readout —
superposition in, survivors out, quantified loss over the integer closure.
The audit answers "what did this cost?" for any compression on the page.

**F12 — Rigidity Rail.** Per-lane rigidity coefficients on the left margin
rail, rendered as a vertical spine of ticks. Rigid lines (high structural
fixity) show dense ticks; fluid lines show sparse ones. The rail is the
reader's at-a-glance map of where the page will resist manipulation.

**F13 — Proximity Axis HUD.** The speaker-position instrument: a three-pole
axis (address / speaker / absent) on the left rail showing each lane's blend
as a marker. As focus moves between lanes, the marker glides. Sudden jumps
flag perspective shifts before the reader consciously registers them.

## Deep interactions (F14–F17)

### F14 — Perspective & Axis Tracking

*How a reader navigating line 4 versus line 12 experiences a sudden change in
background tint, depth-of-field compression, and multi-voice row splitting
when the speaker perspective shifts across the Proximity Axis HUD.*

The reader's focus rests on line 4: the lane is stable, axis marker sitting
near the absent pole, background neutral, all 15 lanes at rest depth. The
reader advances. On line 12 the ledger records a high viewpoint-axis delta —
a perspective ignition.

The canvas responds in three synchronized channels over roughly half a second.
First, **background tint**: the page field behind line 12 warms (toward the
address pole) while the surrounding lanes cool slightly, so the shift reads
as a local climate change, not a highlight. Second, **depth-of-field
compression**: lanes 11 and 13 soften and recede, line 12 sharpens and lifts
toward the reader — attention becomes a physical plane. Third, **multi-voice
row splitting**: line 12 forks. The lane divides horizontally into two
sub-rows — the before-voice and the after-voice — separated by the seam fault
line, each sub-row carrying its own axis tint. The reader is now looking at
two chambers sharing one wall.

The Proximity Axis HUD narrates the event: the marker visibly travels the
axis during the transition, leaving a fading trail. Dismissal is a tap on
the seam: the sub-rows merge, tint neutralizes, depth releases. The reader
has not just read a shift; they have watched perspective become geometry.

### F15 — Non-Linear Path Planning (Echo-Trajectory)

*How a double-tap on a root word in line 2 draws an active Echo-Trajectory
across lines 7, 11, and 15, changing page navigation into a radial orbit
rather than a vertical scroll.*

The reader double-taps a root word on line 2. The word pulses once — a single
expanding ring — and the page exhales: lines 7, 11, and 15 each raise a small
beacon where the same root occurs. Then the trajectories draw: three curved
filaments arc from the tapped word through the inter-row substrate to the
beacons, drawn in sequence (nearest first), each arriving with a soft tick on
the margin rail.

At the third arrival, the canvas transitions to **radial orbit mode**. The
tapped word becomes the hub: the 15 lanes bend — visually, gently — into
concentric arcs around it, the page bound holding rigid while the interior
re-geometries. Vertical scroll is suspended; navigation is now orbital. A
drag rotates the orbit; tapping any beacon jumps focus to that occurrence,
the hub trailing a thread behind it so the return path is never lost.

The orbit persists as a mode, announced by a thin orbital ring on the margin
rail. An outward fling from the hub dismisses it: arcs relax back into
parallel lanes, filaments fade, scroll resumes. The reader's mental model has
shifted from "where am I on the page" to "what is connected to this word."

### F16 — Temporal De-synchronization (Inverse Path-Trace Lock)

*The mechanical behavior of the Inverse Path-Trace Lock: how crossing a deep
chronological boundary layer on line 8 locks forward progression, forcing a
physical reverse-gesture over the preceding lines to re-sync the system state.*

The reader scrolls forward and reaches line 8, which the timeline rail marks
as a deep chronological boundary — a stratum break. The page resists: forward
motion meets a detent (a haptic stop, a visible compression of the lanes
ahead as if pressed against glass). A lock glyph appears on the left margin
rail beside line 8, and the timeline checkpoint for the boundary illuminates.
The system state is now de-synchronized: the reader's position has outrun
their anchor.

Nothing forward will work — taps ahead land dead, the page will not advance.
The only valid gesture is **reversal**: the reader drags backward, upward
over the preceding lines. As the reverse-gesture travels, the Rollback Lens
engages automatically: the backward trail illuminates as a dotted line, and
the operations being discarded render as struck-through ghosts that dissolve
as the gesture passes them. The reader is physically re-walking the input
trail, in reverse, until the lost anchor is recovered — marked by the lock
glyph releasing with a soft chime and the timeline checkpoint settling.

Only then does forward motion unlock, and the lanes ahead release their
compression with a visible exhale. The lesson is kinesthetic: synchronization
is restored not by pushing forward but by retreating exactly. After several
such locks, readers begin to *feel* boundary layers approaching — the rail's
checkpoint glow becomes a sense, not a signal.

### F17 — Substrate Invariance Gestures (Rasm Reveal)

*How a multi-finger pinch across the 15 rows dissolves modern vocalization
markers instantly, exposing the bare dotless structural skeleton on a clean
gray canvas to demonstrate self-healing data layers.*

The reader places two fingers across the 15 rows and pinches inward. The
vocalization markers — the entire layer of dots, vowel marks, and
reading aids — begin to dissolve: each mark shrinks and fades over roughly
half a second, row by row from the pinch epicenter outward, like frost
melting. What remains is the bare dotless skeleton on a clean gray canvas:
near-black skeletal strokes, generous space, no guidance. The page is
suddenly older and quieter.

The skeleton layer is fully explorable — pan, dwell, trace a stroke with a
finger and feel its path. The margin rails dim to minimal ticks; this is a
contemplative state, and the interface gets out of the way.

Release is the demonstration: the reader spreads their fingers, and the
markers **heal back**. Dot by dot, mark by mark, the vocalization layer
re-attaches with a fine ripple radiating from the release point — each mark
landing exactly where the skeleton's structure dictates. Nothing is
approximated; the layers are separate, and their separation is the proof.
The reader has witnessed substrate independence as a physical event: the
skeleton needs nothing, and the markers are guests that know their seats.

## Page systems (F18–F20)

**F18 — Margin Operating Track.** The margins as a unified instrument: scrub
to move the depth cursor, tap a cell to isolate its lane, long-press a
timeline checkpoint to preview the boundary layer. The track has three
density modes (spine / expanded / full) toggled by pinching the margin
itself. All instrumentation, zero content.

**F19 — Inter-Row Substrate Lens.** The gutter lens with three pressure
speeds: light reveals hidden connections between neighboring lines, firm
reveals skeletal variants beneath the marked text, deep reveals root
continuity threads running vertically through the page. Pressure is read from
touch force / dwell time; each speed has a distinct visual language
(filaments / underlay / strands) so the reader always knows which depth they
are at.

**F20 — Page Bound Equilibrium Governor.** The silent system underneath: keeps
total page height constant while lanes expand and contract, distributes
reclaimed space to the quietest lanes, and enforces the rigid footprint. The
governor surfaces only one UI element — a hairline "breath indicator" on the
margin rail showing how much of the page's space budget is currently claimed
by high-intensity lanes.
